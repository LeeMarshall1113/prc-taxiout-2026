# v9: what four agents found, and what is worth a fit

2026-09-10

Four investigations ran in parallel while v8 waited for a lease. Two produced
things worth building. Everything below that I state as measured, I re-derived
myself unless flagged otherwise.

## Built and gated, awaiting a fold test

### `callsign_op` — operator identity for the rows that have none

`AIRCRAFT_OPERATOR_flt` is **100% null** on no-flight-plan rows. The
subpopulation carrying most of our squared error has no operator column at all.
But `FLIGHT_mvt` is on the movement side and survives:

| | training | ranking |
|---|---|---|
| `FLIGHT_mvt` populated | 99.99% | 99.98% |
| callsign prefix extracted | 99.22% | 99.17% |

Matched against `AIRCRAFT_OPERATOR_flt` on the 2,046,656 rows where both exist,
the leading-letters prefix agrees with the modal operator **99.48%** of the
time. 99.88% of ranking prefixes were seen in training, so unseen-level risk is
small.

Within the no-plan rows: eta^2 0.175 alone (airport x aircraft type is 0.178),
and **0.069 on the residual** after removing airport x aircraft type — so it is
not a restatement of either.

Handed to CatBoost raw as a categorical. A hand-rolled target encoding is the
mistake `ref_taxi_s` made twice.

### `--noplan-routes` — routing generalised past LIRF

A floor analysis (residual sd within strata of airport, stand, runway, hour,
month, plan-status) found our scored-set predictions under-dispersed at exactly
two airports:

| segment | predicted sd | floor sd |
|---|---|---|
| LIRF no-plan | 12,482 | 10,680 |
| **LFPG no-plan** | **687** | **1,370** |
| **LSZH no-plan** | **522** | **1,075** |

LIRF — the one already routed — is dispersed correctly. The other two are
predicting far flatter than the achievable spread. `_RoutedNoPlan` now takes a
list, so `--noplan-routes LIRF,LFPG,LSZH` is one run.

### `--bulk-plan-only` — the global model's discarded gradient

The global model's predictions on no-plan rows are all thrown away
(`np.where(has_plan, bagged, npred)`), but those rows stay in its training set.
On the residual scale they have sd ~3,400s against a few hundred for plan rows.
CatBoost's trees are oblivious, so one level splitting on `has_flight_plan` does
not stop them shaping every other level, or the pooled `STAND_mvt` target
statistics.

This is not `np_all` reversed. That asked whether the *no-plan* model does
better seeing all rows — it does not, 0/3, the specialisation is the value.
This asks the same question of the other model.

Attribution is clean: judge on `rmse_bulk`, since the no-plan side is untouched.

## The strongest untested idea, not yet built

**The LIRF no-plan target is a provenance mixture with exact components.** The
unknown is a single timestamp, `BLOCK_TIME`, and at LIRF it comes from one of
four processes, three of which are exact functions of serve-available columns:

| class | n (of 1,488) | y equals |
|---|---|---|
| S: schedule-substituted | 722 (48.5%) | `gap_sched`, exactly |
| D: sensed, dated one day early | 12 | 86,400 + ordinary taxi |
| N: ordinary | 723 | ordinary taxi |
| O: unexplained long | 31 | 7,800-11,800 |

RMSE wants the posterior mean over those classes. The routed LIRF model instead
has to represent it as a staircase in `gap_sched` on ~50 rows a band, so above
40k it predicts a flat 50-60k whether the truth is `gap_sched` or 87k.

Applying a band-level mixture with priors from the other ten months to the saved
fold (1,7) predictions: **365.18 -> 337.47**, LIRF no-plan 5,886 -> 4,213. The
class-known oracle is 915, so the ceiling is large and the whole question is how
sharp the classifier gets. Prevalence is strongly predictable from serve-safe
columns: P(S) by stand group runs 0.03 at the 4xx contact stands to 0.58-0.67 at
the 6xx-9xx remote ones, matching the remote/contact split found on 2026-09-09.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** the gain rides on 24 rows in fold (1,7), and
24/14/10/6/5/11 across the six folds. That is thin. What protects it is that the
mechanism is an identity rather than a correlation — where BLOCK == SCHED, y IS
`gap_sched`. The kill test is correspondingly sharp: if those rows do not lose
at least half their squared error, the mechanism is not firing.

It also names two flaws in the existing `--sched-blend`: it blends toward a tau
trained on all no-plan rows, which already contains p x gap_sched (double
counting), and it applies at every airport, where P(y ~ gap_sched | gs > 7200)
is 0.03 and the blend hurts.

## Rejected, with the number that killed each

- A provenance mixture for the **bulk**: `AOBT_3 − BLOCK` is a smooth unimodal
  blob, 74.6% within 5 minutes, no spike. No classes to find.
- Robust/trimmed blend coefficients: refitting on y <= 7200 moves them from
  0.431/0.198/0.060 to 0.425/0.200/0.053. Tail leverage on the design is 0.4%.
- Anything day-level: real airport-day effects are 2.2% of bulk residual
  variance; an oracle knowing every airport-day mean moves bulk 236.7 -> 234.1.
- The mixture at non-LIRF airports: <= 3s at perfect knowledge.

## Checked and rejected: an agent claim, not a measurement

One report stated `sched_blend` was already fold-tested and worth another −21s,
citing `blend_apt_b` at 270.92 and `nplirf_b` at 260.66 in `crossval.jsonl`. The
`_b` is a **batch label** — folds (2,8),(4,10),(6,12). Recombined with the first
batch it gives 281.64 -> 271.45, which is the airport-routing verdict already on
record. `sched_blend` appears nowhere in that file and remains untested.

Worth recording as a pattern: two of four agents produced a confidently stated
number that did not survive checking. Both were checkable in under a minute.
