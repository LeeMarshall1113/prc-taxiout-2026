# Two dead ends and the thing that actually matters — 2026-09-04

## Leaderboard

| submission | holdout | leaderboard |
|---|---:|---:|
| v1 (baseline) | 459.23s | **423.12s** |
| v2 (+ airport_plan) | 458.89s | 430.87s |

v2 was 0.33s *better* on the holdout and 7.75s *worse* on the board. Team best

## Dead end 1: the rollover story was wrong

I explained the tail as 24-hour date rollovers in the off-block timestamp.
Measured properly on the LIRF/no-plan group, only **1.2%** of its long rows sit
within an hour of 86,400s and **92% are below 20,000s**, median 7,738s. The
rollover accounts for about a dozen extreme rows across all of 2025. The rest
are, as far as anything in this data shows, genuine multi-hour taxi times at
Rome. Corrections applied to the two earlier notes.

## Dead end 2: modelling P(long) per row

`p = P(y >= 3,000s)` inside the group does vary observably — 0.156 to 0.811 by
hour, peaking at 22:00–00:00, and 0.44 to 0.83 by month. That looked like the
remaining gap. It is not, because the model already has it:

- Across hours inside the group, corr(actual mean, v2 predicted mean) = **0.821**.
- Actual hourly means span 2,336–13,703s; v2 predicts 3,580–15,551s. Same shape.
- Replacing v2's row-level predictions with the **true** hourly means of the
  holdout makes it worse: 458.89s -> 515.00s. A true flat group mean is worse
  still, 542.29s.

An oracle fitted on the answer loses to the model. v2's row-level features
already beat any hour-based rule, so there is nothing to recover here.

## What actually matters: we cannot measure

Bootstrap over the holdout, 400 resamples:

| metric | result |
|---|---|
| v1 RMSE 95% CI | [384, 543] — **159s wide** |
| v2 − v1, paired | −0.79s, CI [−10.8, +7.6], P(v2 better) = 0.54 |
| v2 − v1, worst 100 rows dropped | **+6.97s, CI [+5.44, +8.56], P(v2 better) = 0.00** |

Untrimmed holdout RMSE is a coin flip on a change this size. Trimmed, the same
comparison is unambiguous — and it **agrees with the leaderboard in sign and
magnitude** (+6.97 measured, +7.75 observed) where the untrimmed number had it
backwards.

So `airport_plan` did not do nothing. It made the bulk model measurably worse by
about 7s, and the untrimmed metric was too noisy to show it.

**Select on `validate.trimmed_rmse`; report plain RMSE.** The trimmed metric is
not the competition's, but it is the only one here with enough resolution to
tell two models apart, and it predicted the leaderboard correctly on the one
paired test available.

## Standing question

The leaders are at 277.29. Our flight-plan-present holdout is 278–283s, so their
whole score including tail is below our bulk alone. Neither the group mean
(dead end 1) nor per-row P(long) (dead end 2) explains how. That gap is still
unexplained, and it is not going to be closed by guessing at it — the next
useful step is a better bulk model measured on a metric that can see 5s, not
another theory about the tail.
